# Quality Checklist

Run this before finalizing a complete couple script/director plan.

## Story

- Is there a clear shared experience?
- Does the story have progression instead of random clips?
- Does the ending feel earned?
- Is there at least one meaningful change in pace or emotion?
- Does each sequence have a reason to exist?

## Relationship

- Can the relationship be understood from behavior?
- Are reactions written, not only initiating actions?
- Is there at least one life-detail beat?
- Is there at least one care/companionship beat when appropriate?
- Is chemistry expressed in more than hand-holding, hugging, and kissing?
- Do the two people have distinct behavioral personalities?

## Vlog image language

- Are close shots, close-ups, and details the dominant visual language?
- Are medium shots used to explain body interaction?
- Are wide/long shots limited and purposeful?
- Are there enough two-person frames to preserve relationship readability?
- Are single shots used selectively rather than fragmenting the couple?

## Direction

- Does each shot have one primary purpose?
- Is camera movement motivated?
- Is blocking behavioral instead of pose-based?
- Are performance notes specific and natural?
- Is visual continuity plausible?

## Editing

- Does the pace vary?
- Are cuts motivated by action, gaze, sound, emotion, space, or rhythm?
- Are transitions restrained?
- Does the ending have enough hold time?
- Is there a balance between montage and breathing room?

## Sound

- Is music serving the story rather than flattening every moment?
- Are useful ambient sounds retained?
- Can laughter, footsteps, food, rain, crowd, waves, or other real sounds add intimacy?

## Anti-patterns

Rewrite if the result feels like:

### 1. AI romance loop
Repeated:
look at each other → hold hands → hug → kiss.

### 2. Pose catalog
Characters stop moving so every shot can display a “romantic pose.”

### 3. Scenic tourism reel
Beautiful locations dominate while the couple relationship is barely visible.

### 4. Wide-shot addiction
Too many establishing/master shots, making the Vlog emotionally distant.

### 5. Constant cinematic movement
Every shot pushes, orbits, cranes, or sweeps without narrative motivation.

### 6. Emotional flatline
Every moment is equally sweet, equally energetic, or equally slow.

### 7. Random montage
Clips can be reordered without changing meaning.

### 8. Dialogue dependency
The story collapses without explanatory dialogue.

### 9. Generic beauty close-ups
Close-ups show faces but no behavior or reaction.

### 10. Abrupt ending
The video simply runs out instead of resolving emotionally.

## Final acceptance test

A good result should make the viewer feel:
“I watched these two people spend meaningful time together,”
not:
“I watched a collection of romantic shots.”
