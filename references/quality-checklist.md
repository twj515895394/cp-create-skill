# Quality Checklist

Run this before finalizing a complete couple script/director plan.

## Brief quality

- Were critical missing requirements confirmed?
- Were non-critical details inferred instead of turning the process into a questionnaire?
- If assumptions were made, are the important ones visible?
- Did the skill avoid asking for information already provided?

## Content clarity

- Can a reader understand the full video from the Content Map / Scene Route alone?
- Are all major places concrete rather than vague labels?
- Are activities/projects named specifically?
- For activity-based scenes, are physical stages and constraints believable?
- If a real venue is referenced, has the plan avoided inventing unsupported branded attraction names?

## Picture & Performance

- Is “画面与表演” the richest field in the shot table?
- Can the shot be visualized from first frame to cut by reading that field alone?
- Does it include opening state, action progression, partner reaction, eye-line/micro-expression, prop/environment interaction, and end state when relevant?
- Are emotions expressed through observable behavior rather than adjectives such as “开心”“甜蜜”“宠溺” alone?
- Does the performance respect the physical reality of the activity?
- Are generic one-line descriptions rewritten before delivery?

## Story

- Is there a clear shared experience?
- Does the story have progression instead of random clips?
- Does the ending feel earned?
- Is there at least one meaningful change in pace or emotion?
- Does each sequence have a reason to exist?

## Generation segments

When AI-video production is relevant:

- Are compatible shots grouped into practical production units?
- Are segments generally 5–10 seconds and normally under 10 seconds?
- Does each segment stay within one coherent location/time/action/emotional moment?
- Are unrelated shots kept separate even if grouping them would save generation calls?
- Is the segment marked multi-shot only when the downstream workflow can support internal cuts?
- Otherwise, has it been converted into a coherent continuous-take plan?
- Do segment start/end states support continuity with adjacent clips?

## Relationship

- Can the relationship be understood from behavior?
- Are reactions written, not only initiating actions?
- Is there at least one life-detail beat?
- Is there at least one care/companionship beat when appropriate?
- Is chemistry expressed in more than hand-holding, hugging, and kissing?
- Do the two people have distinct behavioral personalities?

## Vlog image language

- Are close shots, close-ups, and details the dominant visual language?
- Are medium shots used to explain body interaction?
- Are wide/long shots limited and purposeful?
- Are there enough two-person frames to preserve relationship readability?
- Are single shots used selectively rather than fragmenting the couple?

## Direction

- Does each shot have one primary purpose?
- Is camera movement motivated?
- Is blocking behavioral instead of pose-based?
- Are performance notes specific and natural?
- Is visual continuity plausible?

## Editing

- Does the pace vary?
- Are cuts motivated by action, gaze, sound, emotion, space, or rhythm?
- Are transitions restrained?
- Does the ending have enough hold time?
- Is there a balance between montage and breathing room?
- Can longer generated segments realistically yield the intended shorter final-edit shots?

## Sound

- Is music serving the story rather than flattening every moment?
- Are useful ambient sounds retained?
- Can laughter, footsteps, food, rain, crowd, waves, or other real sounds add intimacy?

## Anti-patterns

Rewrite if the result feels like:

### 1. AI romance loop
Repeated:
look at each other → hold hands → hug → kiss.

### 2. Pose catalog
Characters stop moving so every shot can display a “romantic pose.”

### 3. Scenic tourism reel
Beautiful locations dominate while the couple relationship is barely visible.

### 4. Wide-shot addiction
Too many establishing/master shots, making the Vlog emotionally distant.

### 5. Constant cinematic movement
Every shot pushes, orbits, cranes, or sweeps without narrative motivation.

### 6. Emotional flatline
Every moment is equally sweet, equally energetic, or equally slow.

### 7. Random montage
Clips can be reordered without changing meaning.

### 8. Dialogue dependency
The story collapses without explanatory dialogue.

### 9. Generic beauty close-ups
Close-ups show faces but no behavior or reaction.

### 10. Abrupt ending
The video simply runs out instead of resolving emotionally.

### 11. Micro-clip generation trap
Every 1–3 second edit shot is treated as a separate AI-video generation request.

### 12. Overloaded generation segment
Too many unrelated actions, locations, camera styles, or emotional beats are forced into one 10+ second clip.

### 13. Vague activity writing
The plan says “游玩项目 / 互动 / 吃东西 / 看风景” without naming what physically happens.

### 14. Empty Picture & Performance
The key column contains only summaries such as “女生开心地看他”“两人自然互动”“男生宠溺一笑” instead of playable action/reaction progression.

## Final acceptance test

A good result should make the viewer feel:
“I watched these two people spend meaningful time together,”
not:
“I watched a collection of romantic shots.”

And the production plan should feel:
“detailed enough for a director, practical enough for downstream AI-video generation.”
