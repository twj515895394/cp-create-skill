# Scene Library — Entertainment

## Scope

Use for theme parks, amusement parks, zoos, aquariums, arcades, exhibitions, leisure attractions, public shows and similar experience-driven venues.

Do not use this file merely because a scene is "fun." The physical environment and activity pattern should belong to this domain.

## Domain principle

Entertainment venues work best when the video mixes:

- headline experiences,
- navigation / waiting,
- ordinary transitions,
- recovery,
- food / objects,
- shared observation,
- end-of-day state change.

A venue should feel lived through, not sampled as a list of attractions.

## Theme Park / Amusement Park

### Entrance / gate

**Realistic activity**
- pass entry control,
- handle ticket / phone / wristband,
- pause after entering,
- orient toward map or signage,
- decide first direction.

**Visual anchors**
- gate structure,
- ticket/wristband,
- map board,
- directional sign,
- themed façade.

**Useful Motifs**
- prepare,
- choose,
- navigate/search,
- discover.

**Relationship affordances**
- one handles practical entry,
- one gets distracted by the environment,
- one points out the first destination,
- one waits while the other checks belongings.

**State possibilities**
- ticket/map becomes a carried prop,
- route decision establishes who leads.

**AI-friendly notes**
- medium-close two-shot around map/signage,
- over-shoulder map detail,
- hand/object inserts,
- environment scale can be separated into a face-light wide/back view.

### Map / route decision point

**Realistic activity**
- unfold or inspect map,
- point along a route,
- compare signs/phone,
- disagree lightly or change plan,
- move off in a chosen direction.

**Visual anchors**
- folded map,
- phone route,
- finger tracing route,
- directional arrows.

**Useful Motifs**
- choose,
- search,
- tiny disagreement,
- role division.

**State possibilities**
- map stays in hand or becomes folded/crumpled later,
- route choice creates the next scene.

**AI-friendly notes**
- ideal for close/medium-close identity shots because activity is naturally compact,
- avoid requiring readable tiny text as a story-critical detail.

### Queue / waiting zone

**Realistic activity**
- shuffle forward,
- lean on railing,
- check wait time,
- look toward ride,
- adjust bag/clothing,
- react to nearby sounds or movement.

**Useful Motifs**
- wait,
- anticipate,
- observe,
- tease,
- recover energy.

**Relationship affordances**
- one is impatient, one calm,
- one watches the ride while the other watches them,
- practical care can happen without stopping the queue.

**State possibilities**
- anticipation rises,
- object/food may be finished while waiting.

### Ride boarding / restraint

**Realistic activity**
- enter vehicle,
- sit,
- place belongings,
- pull restraint,
- check partner reaction,
- wait for dispatch.

**Physical constraints**
- body movement becomes restricted once restrained,
- hands/props should not behave as if characters can freely walk or turn around.

**Useful Motifs**
- attempt,
- anticipation,
- challenge,
- fear/bravery contrast.

**AI-friendly notes**
- favor medium or medium-close shots before high-speed motion,
- identity-critical reaction can be shown before/after the most complex movement.

### High-energy ride

**Realistic activity**
- limited body movement from restraint,
- wind/hair/clothing response,
- involuntary reaction,
- brief partner glance when physically plausible.

**Useful Motifs**
- challenge,
- contrast,
- surprise,
- shared experience.

**AI risk**
- high motion + two clear faces + complex ride geometry is a compound load.
- consider separating ride spectacle from face-critical reaction.

### Post-ride exit / recovery

**Realistic activity**
- walk out slower,
- fix hair/clothing,
- catch breath,
- laugh at reaction,
- check photo/result if relevant,
- drink water or pause.

**Useful Motifs**
- recover,
- observe,
- tease,
- care.

**State possibilities**
- messy hair,
- fatigue,
- inside joke,
- change in confidence.

This is a high-value place for lived-in Micro Moments.

### Food / snack stop

**Realistic activity**
- read options,
- order,
- receive packaging,
- open,
- first taste,
- share/steal,
- carry while walking,
- discard packaging later.

**Visual anchors**
- cup,
- paper bucket,
- wrapper,
- tray,
- napkin.

**Useful Motifs**
- choose,
- share,
- steal/compete,
- discover preference.

**State possibilities**
- food container continues into the next scene,
- hands become occupied,
- small mess/stain can persist briefly.

### Merchandise / shop

**Realistic activity**
- browse,
- pick up,
- compare,
- try accessory,
- use mirror,
- decide whether to buy,
- leave with bag.

**Useful Motifs**
- choose,
- playful test,
- tiny disagreement,
- record/photo,
- surprise.

**State possibilities**
- shopping bag becomes a visible time-passing prop,
- accessory may recur as callback.

### Rest area / bench

**Realistic activity**
- put bags down,
- sit,
- change posture,
- rub feet/legs,
- drink,
- check map/phone,
- watch people pass,
- reluctantly stand again.

**Useful Motifs**
- recover,
- care,
- wait,
- quiet observation.

**Relationship affordances**
- practical care is stronger here than staged romance,
- one can handle bags/water while the other rests.

**AI-friendly notes**
- static observational medium shots work well,
- hand/object details can carry intimacy without face-heavy action.

### Parade / show viewing

**Realistic activity**
- find viewing position,
- adjust line of sight,
- react to sound/light,
- point something out,
- share attention rather than constantly looking at each other.

**Useful Motifs**
- wait,
- discover,
- synchronized attention.

**Relationship affordances**
- CP feeling comes from looking at the same thing,
- one notices the other's reaction after the event starts.

### Night spectacle / fireworks

**Realistic activity**
- arrive early or join crowd,
- wait,
- light changes,
- both look up,
- reactions become quieter,
- leave or hold on an environmental ending.

**Useful Motifs**
- wait,
- synchronized attention,
- payoff,
- quiet companionship.

**AI-friendly pattern**
- face-critical reaction in medium-close / close,
- then environment-critical wide with back / side-back / silhouette,
- identity and environmental scale do not need to be proved in one shot.

## Zoo / Aquarium

### Night safari / open-vehicle animal drive

**Realistic activity**
- move slowly through a dark animal area while scanning both the route ahead and the sides,
- ease off or pause when an animal crosses or approaches the vehicle,
- alternate between windshield / rear-view environment and passenger reaction,
- let one passenger point, use binoculars or track movement while the driver keeps route control,
- resume the route after the encounter, carrying forward surprise, fatigue or quieter attention.

**Visual anchors**
- open-sided jeep, windshield frame, dashboard, dim path lamps, water or reflective surfaces, animal crossing close to the vehicle.

**Useful Motifs**
- discover / notice,
- synchronized attention,
- environment interrupts.

**Relationship affordances**
- the passenger spots something first and redirects the driver's attention without asking them to abandon control,
- the driver slows or turns while the passenger tracks the animal, then both settle into shared observation.

**State possibilities**
- each encounter changes pace and attention; repeated near encounters can accumulate into a quieter end-of-route sense of wonder.

**AI-friendly notes**
- separate close / medium-close face reactions from animal and environment plates; use rear / side-back two-shots for spatial scale,
- keep vehicle movement simple and let cuts carry animal proximity; avoid requiring two exact faces, a moving vehicle and complex animal interaction in one long take.

Useful subspaces:
- entrance map,
- viewing glass,
- feeding/show schedule,
- dark aquarium tunnel,
- rest bench,
- souvenir shop.

Strong mechanisms:
- synchronized attention,
- pointing / discovering,
- one sees the animal first,
- reflection/glass as visual anchor,
- quiet observation,
- waiting for an animal to move.

AI note:
- reflections, glass and moving animals increase complexity; keep face-critical behavior simple when the environment is visually busy.

## Arcade / Game Venue

Useful subspaces:
- token/card station,
- claw machine,
- rhythm game,
- racing game,
- prize counter,
- rest/drink area.

Strong mechanisms:
- competition,
- failed attempt,
- teaching,
- role reversal,
- prize/state carryover.

Avoid turning every beat into winning. Failure/retry often creates more character behavior.

## Exhibition / Museum / Immersive Show

Useful subspaces:
- entry ticket,
- first room,
- interactive installation,
- quiet viewing,
- shop,
- exit.

Strong mechanisms:
- observation,
- interpretation difference,
- one gets absorbed while the other waits,
- shared photo,
- discovery,
- quiet attention.

## Domain QC

Before using an Entertainment scene:
- Is this more than "they play happily"?
- Does the scene contain a physically specific activity or state?
- Are headline attractions balanced with ordinary time?
- Does at least some state carry forward?
- Are AI-wide shots being used for space rather than unnecessarily for face identity?
