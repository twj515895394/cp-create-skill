# Scene Library — Outdoor Leisure

## Scope

Use for parks, lawns, picnics, cycling, lakeside, riverside, beach, camping, trails, overlooks and other open-air leisure.

Use this domain when **terrain, equipment, open-air movement or outdoor pacing** materially shapes behavior.

Do not route here merely because a scene happens outside:
- outdoor concert/festival → Entertainment when the event/show drives behavior,
- rain/snow/heat → Season / Weather when weather is the main engine,
- travel sightseeing → Travel when journey/arrival/navigation state is the main engine.

## Domain principle

Outdoor realism comes from:

- terrain,
- footing,
- wind / sun / water,
- carried equipment,
- setup / pack-down,
- distance and fatigue,
- slower environmental observation.

Avoid reducing outdoor content to:
> walk → look at scenery → smile.

## Park path / lawn

### Realistic activity
- choose path,
- slow/stop at lawn or bench,
- carry drink/bag,
- watch dogs/people/activity,
- sit on grass,
- adjust pace,
- move between sun and shade.

### Visual anchors
- path,
- lawn,
- bench,
- trees,
- bottle/cup,
- tote/backpack.

### Useful Motifs
- navigate,
- observe,
- recover,
- synchronized attention,
- discover.

### Relationship affordances
- one walks faster then slows,
- one chooses shade,
- one notices something first,
- quiet shared attention works better than constant face-to-face interaction.

### State possibilities
- walking energy → seated rest,
- drink level changes,
- carried items move to ground/bench,
- light/weather can shift.

### AI-friendly notes
- environment can be shown in wide/back views,
- use closer shots for face-critical reactions,
- avoid dense crowds unless needed.

## Picnic

### Realistic activity
- choose spot,
- unfold blanket,
- place food,
- open containers,
- keep items from wind,
- pour drinks,
- share/compare food,
- clean up and repack.

### Visual anchors
- blanket,
- basket/bag,
- containers,
- cups,
- napkin,
- small trash bag.

### Useful Motifs
- prepare,
- share,
- choose,
- environment interruption,
- finish/clean up.

### Relationship affordances
- one sets up while the other handles food,
- one reaches across shared spread,
- one notices unstable cup/container,
- packing up can show natural coordination.

### State possibilities
- packed bag → spread-out objects → repacked bag,
- food decreases,
- blanket/clothing picks up grass/sand.

### AI-friendly notes
- many small objects can overload one shot,
- keep prop ownership simple,
- use inserts for opening/pouring rather than both characters manipulating everything at once.

## Cycling / bike rental

### Realistic activity
- choose/adjust bike,
- check seat/helmet,
- start cautiously,
- match pace,
- stop at crossing/viewpoint,
- park/lock,
- recover after riding.

### Visual anchors
- bicycle,
- helmet,
- lock,
- rental station,
- path markings.

### Useful Motifs
- prepare,
- attempt,
- learn,
- pace mismatch,
- recover.

### Physical constraints
- hands should stay plausibly on controls while moving,
- complex side-by-side riding + face-critical dialogue is high risk.

### AI-friendly notes
- identity before/after ride is safer,
- motion-focused riding shots may relax face precision,
- side/back follow shots work well.

## Lakeside / riverside

### Realistic activity
- walk along edge,
- sit on wall/steps,
- watch water/boats,
- feed or observe wildlife if appropriate,
- hold drink,
- take brief photos,
- adjust clothing because of breeze.

### Visual anchors
- rail/steps,
- waterline,
- bridge,
- bench,
- reflections,
- boats/birds where appropriate.

### Useful Motifs
- observe,
- synchronized attention,
- quiet companionship,
- discover.

### AI-friendly notes
- water reflections and wind add motion,
- use environment shots for scale and close/medium reactions separately.

## Beach / shoreline

### Realistic activity
- remove/put on shoes,
- step around wet sand,
- carry sandals,
- react to cold water,
- sit near shoreline,
- shake sand from clothing/towel,
- collect belongings before leaving.

### Visual anchors
- sandals,
- towel,
- bag,
- waterline,
- shells,
- sunscreen,
- bottle.

### Useful Motifs
- attempt,
- react,
- environment interruption,
- recover,
- clean up.

### State possibilities
- feet/clothes become wet/sandy,
- shoes move to hands/bag,
- hair/clothing changes because of wind.

### AI-friendly notes
- water + wind + moving clothes + exact faces is a compound load,
- split water interaction from face reaction if necessary.

## Camping / campsite

### Realistic activity
- unload gear,
- choose ground,
- unfold/assemble shelter,
- organize chairs/table,
- prepare simple food/drink,
- manage lights,
- pack down later.

### Visual anchors
- tent,
- chair,
- cooler,
- lantern,
- mat,
- bag,
- simple cookware.

### Useful Motifs
- prepare,
- shared task,
- fail/retry,
- environment interruption,
- finish.

### Relationship affordances
- natural division of labor,
- one holds while one fixes,
- failed setup creates low-stakes reaction,
- quiet evening after setup offers contrast.

### AI-friendly notes
- tent assembly is mechanically complex,
- break setup into simple states rather than one long transformation.

## Trail / hike / lookout

### Realistic activity
- adjust straps,
- choose footing,
- pause at incline,
- drink,
- wait for partner,
- check route,
- arrive at viewpoint,
- recover before moving again.

### Visual anchors
- backpack,
- trail marker,
- bottle,
- steps/rocks,
- viewpoint rail.

### Useful Motifs
- navigate,
- challenge,
- recover,
- care,
- synchronized attention.

### State possibilities
- fatigue accumulates,
- pace changes,
- clothing becomes less neat,
- water decreases.

### AI-friendly notes
- uneven terrain + full-body walking + exact faces can be risky,
- use closer rest/reaction shots and wider side/back movement shots.

## Outdoor rest point

### Realistic activity
- put bag down,
- sit/lean,
- drink,
- wipe sweat,
- adjust shoe/strap,
- check route,
- watch environment,
- stand again slowly.

### Useful Motifs
- recover,
- care,
- observe.

### Story value
Outdoor rest is a strong bridge between headline activities and lived-in pacing.

---

## Outdoor Leisure QC

Before using an Outdoor scene:
- Does terrain/equipment/environment actually change behavior?
- Are there physical consequences such as fatigue, wind, water, sand or carrying state?
- Is the scene more than walking and scenery?
- If weather dominates the event, should Season / Weather become Primary Domain?
- If a ticketed attraction/show dominates, should Entertainment become Primary Domain?
- Are face-critical shots separated from complex environmental motion when useful?
