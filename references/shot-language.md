# Vlog Directing and Shot Language

## 1. Vlog framing priority

For the default couple Vlog mode, the dominant image language is intimate.

Recommended priority:

1. **Close shot / close-up / detail**
2. **Medium close-up / medium shot**
3. **Wide / long shot**

The majority of the finished piece should normally live close to the couple.

A practical default starting distribution is:

- Close, close-up, detail, reaction: **45–60%**
- Medium and medium close: **30–40%**
- Wide, full, long, environmental: **10–20%**

Do not treat the percentages as a strict quota. The key rule is:
**wide shots must justify themselves.**

The distribution is useful planning guidance and may be made more specific for a concrete production when focal-length planning matters.

## 2. Directing lenses before choosing technique

These are **creative lenses, not approval rules**.

Before choosing framing, movement or camera style, it can help to ask what kind of experience the shot might create.

### What can this shot contribute?

A shot may:
- reveal an action,
- make a reaction readable,
- establish or distort spatial information,
- expose a relationship shift,
- create anticipation,
- preserve a useful detail,
- hold silence,
- connect one event to another,
- create rhythm,
- create atmosphere,
- become subjective or dreamlike,
- emphasize spectacle,
- or simply provide a deliberate visual pleasure that suits the piece.

Narrative function is useful, but it is not the only valid reason for a shot.

### What can camera movement contribute?

Movement may:
- follow a character,
- discover new information,
- redirect attention,
- change spatial relationship,
- increase or release energy,
- enter or leave a scene,
- support a subjective or emotional shift,
- create rhythm with music or cutting,
- stylize the world,
- or create a deliberately expressive image.

“Motivation” can therefore be narrative, emotional, rhythmic, spatial, subjective or stylistic.

Static observation, handheld instability, precise choreography, orbiting, drifting or other movement styles can all work when they fit the piece. Avoid only the mechanical habit of applying the same movement because it is assumed to look cinematic.

### Relationship is larger than facial reaction

Relationship can be expressed through:
- blocking and distance,
- who leads or follows,
- foreground/background placement,
- screen direction,
- shared or separated space,
- pauses and timing,
- sound bridges,
- reaction timing,
- object handoff,
- edit order,
- or physical intimacy when appropriate.

Use whichever combination the material suggests. These are possibilities to consider, not required layers and not an exhaustive list.

## 3. What close shots are for

Use close framing for:
- face reactions,
- laughter,
- glances,
- tiny changes in expression,
- hands passing objects,
- shared food,
- clothing adjustment,
- small care gestures,
- phone/map/menu interactions,
- playful physical details,
- hair, sleeve, cup, ticket, gift, key object,
- POV-like intimacy.

Close framing creates “I am there with them” Vlog intimacy.

Avoid using close-ups only for beauty display. A close shot should ideally contain behavior, reaction, or relationship information.

## 4. What medium shots are for

Use medium framing for:
- two-person interaction,
- walking and talking,
- light physical play,
- ordering or shopping,
- sitting together,
- cooking,
- casual activity,
- showing enough body language to read relationship dynamics.

Medium shots are the bridge between facial intimacy and spatial clarity.

## 5. What wide and long shots are for

Use sparingly for:
- establishing a new location,
- showing scale needed to understand an activity,
- time-of-day transition,
- visual breathing room,
- chapter transition,
- spatial reset,
- romantic payoff,
- ending punctuation.

A wide shot should usually answer a specific question:
- Where are they now?
- Has time changed?
- How large is this moment compared with them?
- Are we intentionally creating distance after a close sequence?
- Is the environment part of the emotional payoff?

Do not insert wide shots just to make the work “cinematic.”

## 6. Couple composition

Prefer two-person framing when the relationship itself is the subject.

Useful patterns:
- shoulder-to-shoulder two-shot,
- one foreground / one background,
- side-by-side walk,
- one active / one reacting,
- over-shoulder that still preserves the partner,
- shared-object composition,
- close two-shot,
- partial-body intimate composition.

Single shots are useful for:
- reaction emphasis,
- rhythm,
- character contrast,
- one person observing the other,
- brief subjective point of view.

Return to relationship framing before singles fragment the scene too much.

## 7. Camera position

Default camera behavior should feel human and present.

Useful:
- eye-level,
- slightly handheld,
- shoulder-height,
- seated eye-line,
- side-follow,
- front-follow,
- behind-follow,
- over-shoulder,
- natural phone/camera POV.

Use unusual angles only when motivated by:
- an activity,
- a joke,
- a physical movement,
- a subjective moment.

Avoid:
- repeated dramatic low angles,
- excessive overheads,
- constant crane-like movement,
- arbitrary orbit shots.

## 8. Camera movement

### Handheld micro-movement
Best for:
- authentic Vlog energy,
- candid reactions,
- playful interaction.

Keep movement readable; “natural” is not the same as unstable.

### Follow shot
Best for:
- walking,
- chasing,
- entering locations,
- moving from one activity to another.

### Static observational shot
Best for:
- natural action unfolding,
- sitting together,
- eating,
- practical care,
- quiet moments.

### Gentle push / pull
Use only when emotional focus benefits from it.

### Pan / tilt
Use to follow an actual action, not as decoration.

## 9. Blocking

Direct the couple through relational blocking, not isolated posing.

Strong blocking:
- one leads, one follows,
- one turns back,
- one leans closer to inspect something,
- one reaches across frame,
- both move toward the same object,
- one exits and the other catches up,
- asymmetrical positions that resolve into closeness.

Weak blocking:
- both stand still and smile at camera,
- repeated symmetrical poses,
- repeated face-to-face staged romance,
- every shot ending in a hug.

## 10. Focus and depth

For intimate Vlog language:
- prioritize faces, hands, shared objects,
- allow foreground occlusion when it feels observational,
- use shallow depth selectively for emotional emphasis,
- preserve enough environment to maintain context.

Do not blur away the partner when the point of the shot is the relationship.

## 11. Visual continuity

Maintain:
- screen direction,
- walking direction,
- relative character positions,
- hand/object continuity,
- wardrobe and prop continuity,
- time-of-day logic,
- believable location progression.

Intentional discontinuity is allowed for montage, but it should feel rhythmic rather than accidental.
