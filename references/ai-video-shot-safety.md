# AI Video Shot Safety

## Purpose

This reference protects **generation reliability without flattening directing creativity**.

Use it only when AI-video generation, shot feasibility, or generation-segment planning is relevant.

It is not a conventional cinematography rulebook.

## Core principle — Identity / Environment Separation

> The shot that proves who the characters are does not have to be the same shot that proves where they are.

When facial identity matters:
- prefer close / medium-close / controlled medium framing.

When environmental scale matters:
- face identity may be reduced,
- back / side-back / silhouette / partial figure can be stronger and more reliable.

Do not force one shot to carry every visual function.

## Complexity budget

Inspect the combined demand of:

1. character count,
2. facial-identity precision,
3. body/action complexity,
4. camera-motion complexity,
5. environment/crowd/effects complexity,
6. duration and continuity demand.

A shot becomes fragile when several dimensions are simultaneously high.

### Low-complexity patterns

- one or two characters, close/medium-close, simple interaction,
- hand/object detail,
- static/semi-static two-shot,
- wide environmental shot with back/side-back figures,
- simple follow shot without face-critical precision.

### Medium-complexity patterns

- two-person medium/full interaction,
- moderate walking/talking with clear faces,
- simple activity with moving background,
- moderate camera follow plus object handoff.

### High-complexity patterns

- multiple people with individually recognizable faces at distance,
- full-body complex interaction + clear identity + busy environment,
- fast running/dancing/fighting + face lock + camera orbit,
- heavy occlusion/crossing limbs + complex props,
- large ride/vehicle motion + two clear faces + strong camera movement,
- long continuous clip with several unrelated action states.

High complexity is not forbidden. It should earn its risk or be decomposed.

## Face-distance strategy

### Close / close-up
Best for:
- identity,
- expression,
- tiny reaction,
- intimate behavior.

Default risk: low when action is simple.

### Medium-close / medium
Best for:
- two-person interaction,
- body language,
- object exchange,
- walking/standing behavior.

Default risk: low-to-medium.

### Full-body
Use when full-body action is genuinely needed.

Risk increases when:
- both faces must remain exact,
- limbs cross,
- camera rotates,
- environment is busy.

### Wide / long
Best for:
- location,
- scale,
- time change,
- transition,
- breathing room,
- ending punctuation.

For AI video, do not make wide/long shots face-critical by default.

Prefer:
- back view,
- side-back,
- silhouette,
- small non-identifiable figures,
- partial occlusion that feels natural.

## The "do not max all dimensions" rule

Avoid simultaneously demanding:

- two+ exact identities,
- full bodies,
- complicated synchronized action,
- complex moving camera,
- dense environment/effects,
- long duration.

If the idea needs all of them, consider an adjacent-shot solution.

## Functional split patterns

### Pattern A — Environment → Identity

1. wide/back establishing shot,
2. cut to medium-close face reaction.

### Pattern B — Identity → Environment

1. close/medium-close reaction,
2. cut to wide/back payoff.

Useful for:
- fireworks,
- views,
- large attractions,
- concerts,
- travel arrival.

### Pattern C — Action → Reaction → Detail

1. medium action,
2. close reaction,
3. object/hand detail.

Useful when a single generation would otherwise require complex action and perfect identity simultaneously.

### Pattern D — Before / During / After

1. face-clear anticipation before difficult motion,
2. motion-focused shot where identity demand is relaxed,
3. face-clear recovery afterward.

Useful for:
- rides,
- sports,
- fast movement,
- water activities.

## Multi-person rule

As character count rises:
- simplify action,
- simplify camera,
- reduce face-critical distance,
- use staging rather than simultaneous complex gestures.

Do not solve every multi-person scene with a wide frontal lineup.

## Hands / objects

Risk rises with:
- multiple hand-to-hand transfers,
- small deformable objects,
- fingers crossing faces,
- both characters manipulating the same object rapidly.

Prefer:
- one clear handoff,
- stable prop ownership,
- insert shot for important object state,
- simple start/end ownership.

## Camera movement

Camera movement is allowed.

Reduce risk when needed by favoring:
- static observation,
- gentle follow,
- simple pan/tilt,
- limited push/pull.

Use orbit/crane/rapid perspective change only when it adds enough value to justify generation complexity.

## Occlusion

Natural partial occlusion can improve observational realism, but avoid:
- long face disappearance followed by exact identity recovery,
- repeated crossing bodies,
- prop/hand blocking face during identity-critical moments.

## Wide-shot QC

For every AI wide/long shot, ask:

- What function requires this distance?
- Does the viewer actually need to recognize the face here?
- Could the environment be shown with back/side-back figures?
- Is there an adjacent closer shot that already establishes identity?
- Is the shot adding spatial/emotional value rather than "cinematic decoration"?

If identity is unnecessary, remove the identity burden.

## Generation-segment safety

A generation segment should:
- contain one coherent moment,
- have a clear start state,
- have compatible internal actions,
- have a usable end state,
- avoid abrupt location/time/object resets,
- preserve prop ownership and relative positions when continuity matters.

For vehicle-plus-animal or other busy environment encounters, a safer reusable split is: route / space plate → face or reaction shot → subject detail → back / side-back shared view. Let the edit carry the near pass or occlusion instead of demanding one long generation to maintain exact faces, vehicle motion and a complex moving subject simultaneously.

Split a segment when complexity—not only duration—becomes unreliable.

## Repair ladder

When a shot is too risky, repair in this order:

1. remove nonessential simultaneous action,
2. simplify camera movement,
3. reduce identity demand at distance,
4. separate identity and environment functions,
5. split into adjacent shots/segments,
6. only then remove the creative beat entirely.

The goal is to preserve the idea while lowering compound generation load.

## QC severity

### BLOCK
- one segment mixes incompatible locations/times,
- physical action is impossible,
- continuity-critical object ownership contradicts adjacent clips.

### REVISE
- unnecessary wide face-critical shot,
- too many high-complexity dimensions at once,
- multi-person exact identity at long distance without need,
- complex motion could be split without story loss.

### OPTIONAL
- further simplification or extra inserts that may improve reliability but are not required.

## Core directing principle

> Need the face: move closer.  
> Need the world: the face can become secondary.
