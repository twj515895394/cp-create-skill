# Micro Moments — Shared Tasks

## Purpose

Small behaviors that make teamwork, coordination, mistake and completion feel physical.

Use only after the shared task itself is concrete.

## Role division

- one person gathers tools while the other clears space,
- instruction/list stays in one person's hand or nearby,
- items get passed without both reaching at once,
- one waits while the other finishes a precise step,
- roles switch after difficulty becomes obvious,
- one person quietly takes over cleanup while the other finishes.

## Attempt / mistake / correction

- pause after noticing something does not align,
- look back at instructions before undoing work,
- place removed parts together instead of scattering them,
- hold a piece steady while the other adjusts,
- compare finished result from a small distance,
- test once, then tighten/fix only what failed.

## Packing / organizing

- create temporary piles,
- move one item from "take" to "leave",
- reopen a bag after remembering something,
- compress contents with forearm/body weight,
- label or group items before putting them away,
- leave frequently used items outside until last.

## Cleaning / setup

- move objects to one side before wiping,
- hand over cloth/tool,
- stop to decide where an item belongs,
- discover an old object and briefly inspect it,
- sit/rest only after one clear zone is finished,
- collect packaging/trash as a final step.

## Carrying / moving

- shift box/bag grip,
- set item down before opening a door,
- one walks backward while guiding placement only when physically plausible,
- check corner/clearance before carrying through narrow space,
- place heavy item first, then adjust position in smaller increments.

## Completion

- step back to inspect result,
- test drawer/shelf/device once,
- collect leftover screws/tape/tools,
- flatten packaging,
- take quick photo only after task is actually finished,
- sit among completed work with lower energy.

## Use rule

Shared-task Micro Moments should reveal **coordination and changing task state**.

Do not add unrelated romance gestures that interrupt the work.
