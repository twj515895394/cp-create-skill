# Character Behavior Engine

## 1. Purpose

Character descriptions such as “稳重 / 灵动 / 温柔 / 高冷 / 活泼” are useful starting points, but they do not generate scenes by themselves.

Translate personality into **behavioral tendencies under situations**.

The goal is not to lock characters into rigid archetypes. It is to give the story a reliable source of action, contrast and surprise.

## 2. From adjective to behavior

Weak:

- 男生稳重
- 女生活泼

Stronger:

- when something unfamiliar appears, she usually tries first while he observes before joining,
- when the situation becomes chaotic, he instinctively handles practical details,
- she reacts immediately and visibly; he often reacts one beat later,
- she turns boredom into play; he pretends not to participate, then quietly joins,
- he rarely initiates overt affection, so a late voluntary action carries more weight.

The behavior is more useful than the adjective because it can generate events.

## 3. Behavior dimensions

Use only the dimensions that matter to the brief.

Possible dimensions:

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Initiative | Who starts things? Who follows? |
| Novelty response | Who tries first? Who studies first? |
| Risk response | Who boasts, hesitates, freezes, laughs, protects, retreats? |
| Practical role | Who handles maps, bags, tickets, water, timing, cleanup? |
| Play style | Teasing, competition, imitation, hiding, mock annoyance, deadpan humor? |
| Care style | Direct help, quiet preparation, waiting, noticing fatigue, giving space? |
| Attention style | Environment-first, partner-first, task-first, distracted, observant? |
| Emotion timing | Immediate, delayed, suppressed, exaggerated, private? |
| Social energy | Leads through crowds, follows, avoids attention, performs for camera? |
| Recovery style | Laughs it off, fixes appearance, complains, gets quiet, retaliates playfully? |

Do not fill every row for every project.

## 4. Behavior engine card

For a story with distinctive characters, a compact internal card can help:

- **Default mode:** what this person normally does.
- **When excited:** what changes.
- **When uncomfortable / challenged:** what leaks through.
- **Play pattern:** how they tease or respond to teasing.
- **Care pattern:** how they notice or help.
- **Practical role:** what they naturally handle.
- **Contradiction:** what makes them less one-note.
- **Change potential:** what small behavior could meaningfully shift by the ending.

This card is optional as visible output. It is primarily a story-generation tool.

## 5. Contrast creates scenes

Useful contrast can come from:

- impulsive / deliberate,
- expressive / restrained,
- adventurous / cautious,
- competitive / indifferent-until-provoked,
- distractible / route-focused,
- image-conscious / carefree,
- easily tired / quietly attentive,
- dramatic / deadpan,
- curious / skeptical.

Contrast does not need conflict.

A difference in timing is often enough:
one acts → the other notices later → the second response creates the scene.

## 6. Preserve contradiction and range

Behavior patterns are tendencies, not obligations.

A restrained person **might** unexpectedly become competitive.
A playful person **might** become quiet when tired.
A cautious person **might** be the brave one in one specific situation.
A practical person **might** make one irrational choice.

Do not insert a reversal merely to prove that the character has depth. Instead, keep this possibility available when the scene, relationship or environment naturally brings out another side.

The goal is psychological readability with room for surprise, not mechanical consistency and not mandatory contradiction.

## 7. Character-specific scene design

When selecting an activity, ask:

> What does this situation make **these two characters** do that a generic couple would not?

A ride, café, shop, street or home kitchen is only useful if it creates behavior.

The same location should produce different scenes for different couples.

## 8. Relationship progression through behavior

Do not require physical intimacy to show progression.

Progression can be:

- one stops waiting for permission,
- one learns the other's rhythm,
- teasing becomes easier,
- practical coordination becomes automatic,
- one notices fatigue before being told,
- one voluntarily joins something they rejected earlier,
- a private joke reappears,
- leadership switches,
- silence becomes comfortable.

This often produces stronger CP feeling than simply increasing hand-holding, hugs or eye contact.

## 9. Freedom rule

The behavior engine should generate possibilities, not imprison the characters.

Treat all behavior descriptions as:
- likely tendencies,
- useful starting points,
- sources of contrast,
- or hypotheses the scene can confirm, complicate or overturn.

A scene does not need to preserve the expected pattern, and it does not need to break it either.

If a more interesting and believable choice appears, follow the scene.

Consistency means psychological readability across the whole piece, not repeating the same response in every moment.
